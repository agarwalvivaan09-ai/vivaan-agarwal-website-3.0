01The internship

Four weeks inside a manufacturing business.

I spent four weeks at Howrah Forgings, where I was exposed to the company's manufacturing operations and industrial supply businesses.

A major part of the experience involved learning about elastomeric pads used in Indian Railways, including how they are manufactured, supplied and integrated into railway applications.

I also spent time understanding the company's spring vertical, which gave me a broader view of how a manufacturing business operates across different industrial product lines.

The internship was not limited to one department or one product. It gave me an opportunity to see how manufacturing, sourcing, quality requirements and customer relationships come together in an industrial business.

02An interest beyond the internship

Manufacturing became interesting when I could see it up close.

I became particularly interested in the elastomeric pad business because manufacturing was not completely unfamiliar to me. My family is involved in steel manufacturing, so I had already been exposed to the broader industrial side of business.

Seeing another manufacturing operation from the inside made me curious about where products were sourced, what machinery was required and where smaller manufacturing opportunities existed within a larger supply chain.

That curiosity continued after I noticed an opportunity around the transition from the older Type A pad design toward the newer Type B design.

03The opportunity

A change in product design created a question about supply.

During my time studying the elastomeric pad business, I learned about the transition toward the newer Type B design and the search for suppliers capable of meeting the required quality and specifications.

This made me curious about the manufacturing opportunity behind the product rather than simply the finished pad.

I began looking into what exactly was required to produce the component, what the different parts consisted of, what machinery would be needed and what producing the components locally could potentially cost.

04Product report

Turning an observation into a commercial analysis.

I prepared a product report on the Type B pad component, researching its construction, production requirements, material requirements, machinery and the economics of producing the component.

An important part of the report was understanding that the elastomeric pad was assembled around three individual metal plates. Rather than looking only at the finished rubber pad, I explored whether those plates could be manufactured independently and supplied to the pad manufacturer.

I also worked through the estimated costs involved in producing the components, including the manufacturing requirements and machinery involved. The aim was not to conduct a formal technical feasibility test, but to understand whether the opportunity made commercial and manufacturing sense.

05Product report

From product research to cost analysis.

I prepared a product report examining the Type B pad components, their construction, production requirements, machinery and the estimated economics of manufacturing them.

A key part of the report was a cost analysis covering the expected manufacturing requirements and the potential cost of producing the three individual metal plates.

The complete product report is currently under development and will be added here once finalized.

06From research to business

The report led me to a business I already knew.

After preparing the report, I took it to my father and discussed whether the manufacturing opportunity was something our family business could undertake.

Calcutta Steel Industries already had the relevant manufacturing capabilities to produce the three individual metal plates required for the component. The opportunity therefore became more specific: instead of trying to manufacture the complete elastomeric pad, we could potentially supply the metal components to the company responsible for the rubber moulding and assembly.

This was where the project moved from research into an actual business conversation.

07The connection

Connecting an existing manufacturing capability with a new demand.

Howrah Forgings was looking for a new supplier for the relevant components, while Calcutta Steel Industries had the ability to manufacture the required metal plates.

I helped establish the connection between the two businesses and presented the opportunity based on the research and product report I had prepared.

The formal commercial discussions, negotiations and business dealings were handled by my father. My role was to identify the opportunity, research the product, prepare the report and help bring the two sides together.

08Outcome

From a four-week internship to a real manufacturing opportunity.

What began as something I noticed during a four-week internship eventually became a practical supplier opportunity for Calcutta Steel Industries.

The business subsequently began manufacturing and supplying the Type B components to Howrah Forgings.

I did not handle the formal commercial negotiations or the technical certification process. What I did was identify an opportunity, understand the product, research the production requirements, prepare a commercial product report and connect an existing manufacturing capability with a genuine supply need.

09What i learned

Manufacturing opportunities are often hiding in plain sight.

The most valuable part of the internship was learning to look at a manufacturing business not only as a collection of machines and products, but as a network of suppliers, customers, specifications and production constraints.

I also learned that an opportunity does not necessarily require building an entirely new product. Sometimes the opportunity is to understand an existing supply chain well enough to identify where a capable supplier can enter it.

For me, this was the point at which an internship stopped being purely observational and became an opportunity to apply what I had learned to a real manufacturing business.
