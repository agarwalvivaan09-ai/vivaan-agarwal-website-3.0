FINANCE · SOFTWARE · SYSTEMS

Arbor

A financial operating system designed to bring financial activity, assets, liabilities, goals, investments and analysis into one place.

Created and developed by Vivaan Agarwal

THE IDEA

From tracking money to understanding financial position.

Arbor began with a simple question: what would a financial system look like if it did more than record transactions?

Instead of treating income, expenses, assets, liabilities and financial goals as separate pieces of information, Arbor brings them together into a single financial workspace.

The broader goal is to make financial health easier to understand while creating a foundation for deeper financial modelling and analysis.

THE PROBLEM

Most finance tools tell you what happened.

Traditional financial tracking often focuses on transactions, balances and spending history.

Arbor is designed to connect those records to the larger questions behind financial decision-making:

  • How financially resilient am I?
  • What happens if my income changes?
  • How much debt am I carrying?
  • How does my net worth change over time?
  • Can I realistically reach a financial goal?

FINANCIAL DASHBOARD

One view of the financial system.

The Arbor dashboard acts as the central financial workspace. Users can record transactions, add assets and liabilities, configure tax settings and create financial goals.

These inputs feed into a broader financial overview covering income, expenses, net cash flow, cash balance, total debt, monthly debt payments, disposable income and net worth.

HOUSEHOLD FINANCIAL RESILIENCE INDEX · IMPLEMENTED

Measuring more than wealth.

A central idea behind Arbor is that financial health cannot be understood through wealth alone.

The Household Financial Resilience Index (HFRI), my independent research project, has been implemented into Arbor. It scores a financial position from 0 to 100 across four dimensions: savings strength, debt sustainability, emergency coverage and expense stability, using the same published scoring model as the research.

The underlying question is not simply: "How much money do I have?" It is: "How well can my financial position withstand change?"

HFRI was tested on simulated households rather than real ones, and the research is open about its limits. The full method, paper and a standalone calculator are published alongside it.

QUANTITATIVE MODELLING

Modelling uncertainty rather than ignoring it.

Arbor's analytical architecture includes quantitative modelling and scenario analysis designed to explore how financial outcomes can change under different assumptions.

The broader system includes work around probabilistic forecasting, Monte Carlo simulation, inflation and tax logic, financial projections and scenario modelling.

RISK & SHOCK MODELLING

What happens when circumstances change?

Financial plans often look reasonable under normal assumptions. Arbor is designed to examine how financial resilience changes when those assumptions are disrupted.

Income Shock

Examining the effect of changes in income on financial stability.

Expense Shock

Examining the effect of unexpected increases in expenses.

Inflation Shock

Exploring how changing inflation assumptions affect future financial outcomes.

Revenue Shock

Extending the framework to examine variability in business revenue.
