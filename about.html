ABOUT

Vivaan Agarwal

Student of economics, finance and mathematics, interested in understanding how ideas behave when they meet the real world.

I’m a Class XII student at La Martiniere for Boys, Kolkata. My academic interests sit primarily around economics, finance, business and entrepreneurship, with mathematics increasingly becoming an important part of how I approach those subjects.

What interests me about economics is not simply learning models and theories. I’m more interested in what happens when those ideas meet actual people, businesses, markets and institutions. Why do incentives produce certain outcomes? Why do policies work in some situations and fail in others? And why can something that looks sensible on paper behave very differently in practice?

That curiosity has gradually pushed me towards research. At Nikore Associates, through the Young Economist Programme, I studied India’s industrial policy, Production Linked Incentive schemes and the country’s $99.2 billion trade deficit with China. I worked with Ministry of Commerce data and examined how dependence on Chinese components interacts with domestic manufacturing capacity.

I’ve also had the opportunity to conduct primary field research in rural Purulia, studying healthcare, education and infrastructure. Working with field data and constructing an index to examine access and inequality taught me something that textbooks cannot: economic questions often become much more complicated once you encounter the people and conditions behind the data.

Outside formal research, I like learning by building. Arbor is my attempt to bring together several of my interests: finance, mathematics, technology and quantitative modelling. I’m developing it as a modular financial operating system incorporating accounting, investment analytics, financial planning, market data, Monte Carlo simulation, probabilistic forecasting and financial resilience analysis.

Entrepreneurship has given me a different kind of education. Through ElevateX, I built and led a student-led nonprofit, developing its organisational structure, departments, partnerships and community programmes. One part of that work, Project Unnati, focused specifically on financial literacy, delivering workshops on investing, budgeting and banking to students.

I’ve also tried to understand business from closer to the ground. During my work with Howrah Forgings, I studied a manufacturing opportunity created by Indian Railways’ transition to Type B elastomeric pad plates. I developed a feasibility study, researched the machinery required for local production and took the proposal to Calcutta Steel Industries, where I helped assess whether the components could be manufactured.

Writing ties much of this together. I’ve published long-form pieces on economics, finance, markets, entrepreneurship and leadership, while also writing detailed research reports on projects such as Arbor and the Household Financial Resilience Index (HFRI). Writing forces me to turn an idea that exists in my head into something that another person can actually examine.

I don’t have a fixed picture of what I want to be decades from now. What I do know is that I want to keep working at the intersection of economics, finance, mathematics and entrepreneurship, preferably in environments where I have to think carefully, build things and deal with problems that don't have obvious answers.
